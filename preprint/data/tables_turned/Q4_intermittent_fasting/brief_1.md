

# Intermittent Fasting vs. Regular Dieting for Weight Loss

**Question:** Is intermittent fasting better than regular dieting for losing weight? · **Context:** Choosing a diet plan to lose about 20 pounds this year.

---

## The short answer

For most people, intermittent fasting and traditional calorie-cutting produce **nearly identical weight loss**. The largest meta-analysis (99 trials, 6,582 people) found only minor differences, with one exception: alternate-day fasting edged out continuous dieting by about 1.3 kg (~3 lbs) in shorter trials [PMID: 40533200]. One well-designed 12-month trial found that a 4:3 fasting schedule (eat very little 3 days/week) beat daily calorie restriction by about 6 lbs, possibly because it reduced binge eating [PMID: 40163873]. Bottom line: pick whichever approach you can actually stick with for a full year.

## Key findings

- **Overall, fasting ≈ regular dieting.** The largest network meta-analysis found all fasting types and continuous restriction produced similar weight loss vs. each other, with differences generally under 2 kg [PMID: 40533200]. A second meta-analysis of 24 trials agreed [PMID: 36349432].
- **Alternate-day fasting may have a slight edge.** It was the only fasting type to outperform continuous restriction (by ~1.3 kg), but this advantage disappeared in trials lasting 6+ months [PMID: 40533200] [PMID: 36349432].
- **One 12-month trial favored 4:3 fasting.** Participants fasting 3 days/week lost ~2.9 kg more than daily dieters, with less binge eating and uncontrolled eating [PMID: 40163873] [PMID: 40733010].
- **Short-term fasting may burn slightly more fat.** One meta-analysis found fasting produced about 1 kg more fat loss than continuous restriction in the short term, but this didn't hold long-term [PMID: 39458528].
- **A 5:2 plan with meal replacements showed strong results in diabetics**—9.7 kg lost in 16 weeks—but this combined fasting with meal replacements and was only studied short-term in people with type 2 diabetes [PMID: 38904963].
- **Night shift workers saw no difference** between 5:2 fasting and continuous restriction at 24 weeks [PMID: 40554393].

## What is unknown

- **Long-term data (1+ years) is scarce.** Only one provided trial lasted 12 months [PMID: 40163873]. Whether fasting advantages persist or fade beyond a year is unclear [PMID: 40533200].
- **Adherence is the wild card.** Compliance was high in short trials (>80%), but real-world stickability over a full year—which matters most for losing 20 lbs—is poorly studied [PMID: 36349432].
- **Who benefits most?** There's little data on whether fasting works better for specific people (by age, activity level, or eating patterns).

## Confidence: Medium

Multiple large meta-analyses agree on the core finding (no major difference), but long-term trials are few and short-term advantages for fasting are small and inconsistent. The one positive 12-month trial is a single study with limited generalizability.
