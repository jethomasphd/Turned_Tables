# Is Ozempic (Semaglutide) Safe for Weight Loss?

**Question:** Is Ozempic safe? · **Context:** Doctor suggested semaglutide for weight loss; wants to understand risks before starting.

---

## The short answer

Semaglutide is broadly considered safe for weight loss, with an overall favorable risk-benefit profile, but it is not side-effect-free [PMID: 34305810]. The most common problems are gut-related — nausea, diarrhea, constipation — which are usually mild-to-moderate and fade over time [PMID: 33567185]. A large cardiovascular trial (17,604 people, ~3 years follow-up) showed it actually *reduced* heart attacks and strokes by 20% in people with preexisting heart disease [PMID: 37952131].

## Key findings

- **Gut side effects are the main issue.** Across trials, gastrointestinal complaints occur in ~75–83% of semaglutide users vs. ~42–64% on placebo. Most are mild-to-moderate nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, or constipation that improve over weeks [PMID: 33567185; PMID: 36216945; PMID: 33625476].
- **Some people quit because of side effects.** About 3–7% of users in trials stopped treatment due to GI problems, compared to <1% on placebo [PMID: 33567185; PMID: 33625476]. Real-world dropout rates are higher (20–50% in the first year), driven partly by side effects and cost [PMID: 40196933].
- **Serious adverse events are higher than placebo but still uncommon.** A meta-analysis found the risk of serious events (mainly gallstones, pancreatitis) was ~1.6× higher with semaglutide [PMID: 36578889]. The comprehensive safety review found increased gallbladder disease (cholelithiasis) as a real concern [PMID: 34305810].
- **Cardiovascular safety is strong.** The SELECT trial showed a 20% reduction in major cardiovascular events (heart attack, stroke, cardiovascular death) over ~40 months — a significant benefit, not just neutral safety [PMID: 37952131].
- **No confirmed link to thyroid or pancreatic cancer in humans so far**, though animal studies raised a thyroid cancer signal. Low incidence makes definitive conclusions impossible at this time [PMID: 34305810; PMID: 40196933].
- **Weight regain is significant after stopping.** Participants regained about two-thirds of lost weight within one year of discontinuation, suggesting long-term use is needed to maintain results [PMID: 35441470].

## What is unknown

- **Long-term safety beyond 3–4 years** has not been established in obesity populations. Most trials run 1–2 years [PMID: 40196933].
- **Rare outcomes** — thyroid cancer, pancreatic cancer, retinal disease, mental health effects — need more data; current evidence is reassuring but not conclusive [PMID: 34305810; PMID: 40196933].
- **Individual risk factors** (e.g., history of gallstones, pancreatitis, or retinopathy) may change the calculus and aren't well-studied in weight-loss-specific trials.

## Confidence: **Medium-High**

Based on multiple large RCTs (including one with 17,604 participants and ~3 years follow-up), a systematic review of 26 trials, and a comprehensive safety review. GI side effects and gallbladder risk are well-established. Very rare harms remain uncertain due to insufficient long-term data.
