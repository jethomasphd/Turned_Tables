# Does Apple Cider Vinegar Help With Weight Loss?

**Question:** Does apple cider vinegar help with weight loss? · **Context:** Seen it all over social media; deciding whether it is worth buying.

---

## The short answer

There is some evidence that apple cider vinegar (ACV) can produce modest, short-term weight loss — roughly a few pounds over 8–12 weeks — especially in people who are already overweight or have type 2 diabetes [PMID: 41010525] [PMID: 19661687]. However, the effect sizes are small, the study quality is generally low, and recent critiques have flagged statistical problems in key trials, casting doubt on whether the results are trustworthy [PMID: 40771528] [PMID: 39882285].

## Key findings

- **A 2025 meta-analysis of 10 trials (789 people)** found that daily ACV intake significantly reduced body weight, BMI, and waist circumference compared to placebo. Benefits were clearest at ~30 mL/day for up to 12 weeks in overweight/obese adults [PMID: 41010525].
- **A 2009 Japanese trial (155 obese adults)** showed that 12 weeks of daily vinegar (15–30 mL) reduced body weight, visceral fat, waist circumference, and triglycerides versus placebo [PMID: 19661687].
- **A separate 2021 meta-analysis (16 trials, 910 people)** found **no significant effect** of acetic acid on BMI or body fat percentage, contradicting the more positive findings above [PMID: 33436350].
- **Two independent critiques** have raised serious concerns about "improbable data" and statistical errors in influential ACV-weight-loss trials, suggesting some positive results may be unreliable [PMID: 40771528] [PMID: 39882285].
- **ACV may modestly improve blood sugar and cholesterol**, which could benefit metabolic health even apart from weight loss [PMID: 34187442].
- **ACV carries a real dental risk.** A case report documented significant tooth erosion from daily ACV consumption [PMID: 23373303].

## What is unknown

- **Long-term effects beyond 12 weeks** have not been studied. Whether any weight lost stays off is entirely unclear.
- **How ACV compares to simply eating less or exercising more** — no study has made this head-to-head comparison.
- **Optimal dose, form (liquid vs. pill), and who benefits most** remain poorly defined; existing studies vary widely in design.

## Confidence: Low to Medium

Multiple meta-analyses exist, but the underlying trials are small, short, and often rated as having high or unclear risk of bias. Independent researchers have flagged data integrity issues in key studies. The overall signal is weak and contested.
