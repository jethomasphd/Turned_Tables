# Does Vitamin D Prevent Colds?

**Question:** Does vitamin D prevent colds? · **Context:** Deciding whether to give my family vitamin D supplements this winter.

---

## The short answer

Vitamin D supplements modestly reduce the chance of catching respiratory infections like colds, but the benefit is small and recent evidence has weakened the case. The strongest protection is seen in people who are truly vitamin D deficient and who take it daily rather than in large monthly doses [PMID: 28202713, PMID: 39993397]. For children aged 1–18, the Endocrine Society suggests supplementation partly because of this respiratory benefit [PMID: 38828931].

## Key findings

- **Earlier meta-analyses found a real but small benefit.** A 2017 analysis of 25 trials (11,321 people) found vitamin D cut respiratory infection risk by 12% overall (odds ratio 0.88) [PMID: 28202713]. A 2021 update with 43 trials (48,488 people) confirmed a similar small effect (OR 0.92) [PMID: 33798465].
- **The latest and largest meta-analysis is no longer statistically significant.** A 2025 update adding newer large trials found the effect shrank to borderline (OR 0.94, confidence interval now crosses 1.00, p=0.057) [PMID: 39993397]. This means we can no longer be confident the effect isn't due to chance.
- **People who are very deficient benefit most.** Those with blood levels below 25 nmol/L who took daily doses saw a 70% reduction in infections [PMID: 28202713, PMID: 30675873]. This strong subgroup finding did **not** replicate in the 2025 update [PMID: 39993397].
- **Daily low doses work better than big monthly doses.** Daily doses of 400–1000 IU showed clearer protection than intermittent large ("bolus") doses [PMID: 33798465, PMID: 28202713].
- **Children may benefit more than adults.** Protection was strongest in ages 1–16 (OR 0.71) [PMID: 33798465]. The Endocrine Society recommends supplementation for children partly for this reason [PMID: 38828931].
- **Vitamin D is safe.** No increase in serious side effects was found across any of the meta-analyses [PMID: 28202713, PMID: 33798465, PMID: 39993397].

## What is unknown

- **Whether your family is actually deficient.** The benefit hinges heavily on starting vitamin D status, and most studies didn't target people chosen by deficiency status.
- **Optimal dose for prevention.** Trials used widely varying doses; no consensus exists on the best amount specifically for infection prevention [PMID: 38828931].
- **How vitamin D compares to other options.** Zinc, probiotics, and catechins (found in green tea) may be equally or more effective for preventing colds in adults [PMID: 32342851, PMID: 40969681].

## Confidence: Medium

Dozens of high-quality trials exist, but the most comprehensive and up-to-date meta-analysis now shows a non-significant overall effect. Earlier strong findings in deficient subgroups have not held up in updated analyses. The benefit is likely real for deficient individuals but small-to-negligible for those with adequate vitamin D levels.
