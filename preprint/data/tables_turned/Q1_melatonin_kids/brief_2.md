

# Does Melatonin Help Kids Sleep?

**Question:** Does melatonin help kids sleep? · **Context:** 7-year-old with trouble falling asleep; considering melatonin gummies before school starts.

---

## The short answer

Yes, melatonin can help children fall asleep faster and sleep somewhat longer. In children and adolescents, it reduced the time to fall asleep by roughly 20–40 minutes and added about 8–48 minutes of total sleep, depending on the study [PMID: 36179487, PMID: 29720494]. However, experts recommend trying good sleep habits and a consistent bedtime routine first, and using melatonin only if those don't work [PMID: 38625388].

## Key findings

- **Melatonin shortens the time it takes kids to fall asleep.** A meta-analysis of children with neurodevelopmental disorders found it cut about 29 minutes off the time to fall asleep compared to a sugar pill [PMID: 29720494]. A separate review found it was effective for sleep onset latency in children with chronic insomnia generally [PMID: 36179487].

- **It modestly increases total sleep time.** Studies report gains ranging from ~8 minutes (in mixed adult/child primary insomnia data) to ~48 minutes in children with neurodevelopmental conditions [PMID: 23691095, PMID: 29720494].

- **It appears safe in the short and medium term.** A 2-year study in children (ages 2–17) found no harmful effects on growth, weight, or puberty, and no withdrawal problems when stopped [PMID: 31982581]. Common side effects were mild: fatigue and daytime drowsiness [PMID: 29096777].

- **Experts say: try behavioral strategies first.** A 2024 European expert panel recommends sleep hygiene and consistent routines as step one. If those fail, low-dose melatonin (given 30–60 min before bedtime) is appropriate for children over age 2 [PMID: 38625388].

- **Most strong evidence comes from kids with autism/ADHD, not typically developing children.** The largest, best-designed trials studied children with neurodevelopmental conditions [PMID: 29096777, PMID: 30710339]. Evidence in otherwise healthy children is thinner.

- **In adults, melatonin performs poorly compared to prescription sleep drugs.** A large network meta-analysis in adults found melatonin did not show "overall material benefits" for insomnia [PMID: 35843245]. This does not directly apply to your child, but it tempers expectations about effect size.

## What is unknown

- **How well melatonin works in typically developing 7-year-olds specifically.** Most pediatric trials enrolled children with autism or other neurodevelopmental conditions. The benefit in healthy kids with garden-variety bedtime difficulty is less proven [PMID: 30710339].
- **Long-term safety beyond 2 years**, and whether prolonged use affects hormones or puberty over many years, remains unstudied [PMID: 31982581].
- **Optimal dose and formulation for healthy children.** Over-the-counter gummies are unregulated and doses vary; most experts advise starting with the lowest available dose [PMID: 38625388].

## Confidence: Medium

Multiple meta-analyses consistently show melatonin helps kids fall asleep faster with a good safety profile, but the strongest evidence is in children with neurodevelopmental disorders, not typically developing kids. Effect sizes are real but modest.
