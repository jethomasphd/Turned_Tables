

# Does Melatonin Help Kids Sleep?

**Question:** Does melatonin help kids sleep? · **Context:** My 7-year-old has trouble falling asleep. Deciding whether to try melatonin gummies before school starts.

---

## The short answer

Yes, melatonin helps children fall asleep faster and sleep somewhat longer. A major meta-analysis found melatonin was significantly effective at reducing the time it takes kids to fall asleep and increasing total sleep time — notably more so in children than in adults [PMID: 36179487]. European pediatric sleep experts recommend it as a second step when good sleep habits and behavioral strategies alone aren't enough, at a low dose given 30–60 minutes before bedtime [PMID: 38625388].

## Key findings

- **Kids fall asleep faster.** Across studies, melatonin reduced sleep onset latency by roughly 7–40 minutes depending on the population and formulation, and modestly increased total sleep time [PMID: 23691095, PMID: 36179487].
- **It works better in children than adults.** The benefit was statistically significant in children/adolescents but *not* in adults with chronic insomnia [PMID: 36179487].
- **Try sleep hygiene first.** Expert consensus says melatonin should only come *after* consistent bedtime routines, limited screen time, and behavioral strategies have been tried [PMID: 38625388].
- **Screen time matters independently.** Reading on a light-emitting device before bed suppresses the body's own melatonin, delays the internal clock, and makes falling asleep harder — cutting screens before bed may help on its own [PMID: 25535358].
- **Short-term safety looks good.** The most common side effects are daytime drowsiness and headache, at rates similar to placebo. A 2-year study in children (mostly with autism) found no negative effects on growth or puberty [PMID: 31982581, PMID: 29096777].
- **Effects are modest, not dramatic.** One large meta-analysis found the average improvement was about 7 minutes faster to fall asleep and 8 minutes more sleep overall — meaningful but not a miracle [PMID: 23691095].

## What is unknown

- **Long-term safety in typically developing children is unstudied.** The 2-year data comes from kids with autism; no equivalent long-term trial exists for otherwise healthy children [PMID: 31982581].
- **Optimal dose for a healthy 7-year-old is unclear.** Most studies used clinical-grade melatonin in controlled doses; over-the-counter gummies vary in actual melatonin content and are not tightly regulated.
- **Whether melatonin addresses the root cause** of your child's sleep difficulty (anxiety, screen habits, circadian timing) is something these studies don't answer.

## Confidence: Medium-High

Multiple meta-analyses and randomized trials consistently show melatonin helps kids fall asleep faster, with good short-term safety. Confidence is not "high" because most rigorous long-term data comes from neurodevelopmental populations, not typically developing children, and OTC gummy dosing is less controlled than what was studied.
