# Does Melatonin Help Kids Fall Asleep?

**Question:** Does melatonin help kids sleep? · **Context:** My 7-year-old has trouble falling asleep. Deciding whether to try melatonin gummies before school starts.

---

## The short answer

Yes, melatonin helps children fall asleep faster and sleep somewhat longer, and it appears safe in the short and medium term. A meta-analysis of 24 trials found melatonin was significantly effective at reducing the time to fall asleep and increasing total sleep time specifically in children and adolescents [PMID: 36179487]. However, European pediatric sleep experts recommend trying good sleep habits and consistent bedtime routines *first*, and using low-dose melatonin only if those steps aren't enough [PMID: 38625388].

## Key findings

- **Kids fall asleep faster.** Across studies, melatonin reduced sleep onset latency (time to fall asleep) by roughly 25–40 minutes compared to placebo in children [PMID: 29096777, PMID: 36179487].
- **Kids sleep a bit longer.** Total sleep time increased modestly — around 8 minutes in a broad meta-analysis of all ages [PMID: 23691095], but more substantially (~58 minutes) in children with neurodevelopmental conditions [PMID: 29096777].
- **Effects are stronger in kids than adults.** One meta-analysis found melatonin was *not* significantly effective in adults with chronic insomnia but *was* effective in children and adolescents [PMID: 36179487]. A European adult insomnia guideline does not recommend melatonin for adult insomnia [PMID: 28875581].
- **Safety profile is reassuring.** A 2-year study in children found no concerning effects on growth, weight, or puberty. Most common side effects were fatigue and daytime sleepiness [PMID: 31982581]. Multiple reviews describe melatonin as well-tolerated [PMID: 33540815, PMID: 31638842].
- **Experts say behavioral strategies come first.** A 2024 European expert panel recommends sleep hygiene and bedtime routines as step one; low-dose melatonin (given 30–60 min before bed) as step two for children over age 2 who don't improve [PMID: 38625388].
- **Most strong evidence is in kids with autism/ADHD, not typically developing kids.** The largest controlled trials studied children with neurodevelopmental disorders [PMID: 29096777, PMID: 36603513]. Evidence in otherwise healthy children exists but is thinner.

## What is unknown

- **Optimal dose and formulation for a typical 7-year-old.** Most rigorous trials used pharmaceutical-grade prolonged-release tablets, not over-the-counter gummies, which vary widely in actual melatonin content [UNWITNESSED from these papers].
- **Long-term effects in typically developing children.** The 2-year safety data come from children with autism [PMID: 31982581]; comparable long-term data for healthy kids are lacking.
- **Whether stopping melatonin leads to rebound sleep problems.** One study showed some decline after withdrawal but not back to baseline [PMID: 31982581]; more data are needed.

## Confidence: Medium

Strong evidence that melatonin helps children fall asleep faster, but the best-controlled trials are mostly in kids with autism or ADHD, not typically developing children. Safety data are reassuring but limited beyond 2 years. The recommendation to try sleep hygiene first is consistent across sources.
