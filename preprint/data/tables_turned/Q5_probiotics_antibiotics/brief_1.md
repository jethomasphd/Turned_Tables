

# Do Probiotics Prevent Antibiotic-Associated Diarrhea in Children?

**Question:** Do probiotics prevent diarrhea from antibiotics? · **Context:** My child was just prescribed amoxicillin and I'm wondering whether to give a probiotic too.

---

## The short answer

Yes, probiotics significantly reduce the chance of antibiotic-associated diarrhea (AAD) in children. A large Cochrane review of 33 trials (6,352 kids) found probiotics cut AAD roughly in half — from 19% down to 8% — meaning you'd need to give about 9 children probiotics to prevent one case of diarrhea [PMID: 31039287]. **However**, the benefit depends on the probiotic strain, the dose, and how disruptive the antibiotic is. One well-designed trial found that when kids took narrow-spectrum antibiotics (like plain amoxicillin) and had very low diarrhea rates to begin with (~2%), a probiotic added no benefit [PMID: 41699149].

## Key findings

- **Overall, probiotics work.** Pooled data from 63 trials (11,811 participants, adults and children) showed a 42% relative reduction in AAD risk (NNT ~13) [PMID: 22570464]. The pediatric-specific Cochrane review found an even larger effect (55% reduction, NNT 9) [PMID: 31039287].
- **Higher doses work better.** At ≥5 billion CFU/day, AAD dropped from 23% to 8% (NNT 6). Lower doses showed a smaller, borderline benefit [PMID: 31039287].
- **Best-studied strains in children: *Lactobacillus rhamnosus* GG and *Saccharomyces boulardii*.** European pediatric guidelines give both a strong recommendation based on moderate-quality evidence [PMID: 26756877]. A large trial also supports *L. reuteri* DSM 17938 specifically in children on amoxicillin-clavulanate [PMID: 40488914].
- **If the antibiotic barely disrupts the gut, probiotics may not help.** In a trial where children received short-course, narrow-spectrum antibiotics (including amoxicillin), diarrhea rates were only ~2% and the probiotic (*B. lactis* BB-12) did nothing extra [PMID: 41699149]. This is an important nuance for your situation with plain amoxicillin.
- **Probiotics appear safe in otherwise healthy children.** Across 24 pediatric trials, no serious adverse events were attributed to probiotics. Minor side effects (gas, rash, nausea) occurred at similar rates in probiotic and placebo groups [PMID: 31039287].
- **Benefit is clearest when baseline diarrhea risk is moderate-to-high** (e.g., broad-spectrum antibiotics, longer courses, hospitalized children). Studies with low baseline risk showed little or no benefit [PMID: 34385227] [PMID: 41699149].

## What is unknown

- **Whether plain amoxicillin (narrow-spectrum) causes enough gut disruption to warrant a probiotic.** Most positive trials used broader-spectrum antibiotics like amoxicillin-clavulanate. Evidence for amoxicillin alone is thin [PMID: 41699149].
- **Optimal duration of probiotic use.** Some data suggest longer courses improve gut flora more, but clear pediatric dosing timelines are lacking [PMID: 39467682].
- **Long-term effects on the developing microbiome** from routine probiotic use in children remain unstudied in these trials.

## Confidence: Medium

Strong evidence that probiotics reduce AAD in children overall, but the benefit is most clear with broad-spectrum antibiotics at high probiotic doses. For your specific scenario — a healthy child on plain amoxicillin — the benefit may be small or negligible, since diarrhea risk is already low with narrow-spectrum antibiotics.
